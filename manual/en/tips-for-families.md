# Tips for families

Children learn to use a talking app the way they learn to talk: by seeing and hearing the people around them use it, every day, long before they use it themselves.

## Model, don't test

**Modelling** means tapping words on the device while you talk. You say "Let's go **out**", and tap **go** and **out**.

- Model a word or two at a time; you don't have to tap every word you say.
- There's no need to ask the child to copy you or to quiz them ("Show me *cup*"). Just use it yourself, and let them watch.
- Tap **Model** on the top bar (with the grown-up settings open) so your taps aren't counted as the child's words. Hidden words show faded then, so you can model them before your child has them. It switches itself off two minutes after your last tap.

## Start small

- Pick one routine: snack time, bath time, play. Model the same few words every time ("more", "stop", "go", "all done").
- **Word of the week** in **Daily coaching** helps the whole family model the same words.
- Show new words only after you've modelled them many times. See [Words and folders](words-and-folders.md).

## Wait

After you model, pause for about ten seconds and look at the child, as if you're waiting for their answer. Give them time to answer, with the device, a sound, a gesture or a word. Every way of communicating counts.

## Keep it with the child

The device is the child's voice. Keep it within reach all day: at meals, in the car, in the garden. Lock the settings, never the voice.

## Use every language

Speak your home language with the child, and switch the board to it with one tap. Children use their device much more when it speaks their home language. Every word is in the same place in every language, so switching doesn't mean learning the board again.

## Everyone, the same way

Show grandparents, babysitters and friends how the child uses the app; a two-minute demonstration in person helps more than any leaflet. Agree who changes the board, and tell each other when you do. If words suddenly move, the child can get confused.

## Common worries

**Will this stop my child from learning to talk?** No. Research shows that tools like this don't hold back speech, and may even help. They're a bridge to talking, not a replacement.

**Is my child too young?** There is no minimum age. Using pictures, gestures and speech together early on supports development.

**Does my child need to be "ready" first?** No test of readiness is needed. It works best when grown-ups keep it simple, always available, and use it themselves.

**Why so few pictures of things?** About 25 words make up most of a toddler's talk, and about 200 core words cover most everyday speech. Keeping them in fixed places builds the child's motor memory.
